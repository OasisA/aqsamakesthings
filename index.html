MD student, Stanford School of Medicine · Bioengineer · EMT

I design things for the people who use them at the bedside.

I'm a first-year medical student at Stanford. I studied bioengineering at the University of Pittsburgh, and I've been an EMT with Edison EMS since 2022. Most of my projects start with a clinician describing a problem, from pediatric emergency medicine to anesthesiology to a trauma ICU. They end with something I've built, tested, and usually rebuilt.

How the pieces connect

Four habits that show up in everything I work on

01

Start where care happens

Before my senior design team chose a problem, we ran an ethnography plan at the WISER simulation center. Outside the lab, I've served as an EMT with Edison EMS and volunteered weekly with older hospitalized patients through UPMC's Hospital Elder Life Program.

02

Build rough, test honestly

My strength is fast, low-resolution prototyping. I've built a one-way valve from two syringes in fifteen minutes and taken apart a CPR valve to see how it works. When testing showed our chest tube valve didn't stop backflow, I proposed redesigning it before we froze the design.

03

Make it teachable

Many of my projects are training tools: a pediatric orthopedic reduction simulator we handed off to train incoming residents, and silicone ultrasound simulators for pediatric nerve blocks. As a cell biology teaching assistant, I also helped redesign a course around hands-on modules.

04

Work on the systems around care

As an intern for Pennsylvania Rep. Dan Frankel, chair of the House Health Committee, I researched and drafted legislation on nurse aide career development. With Pitt's Student Government Board, I advocated on Capitol Hill and chaired an anti-hate committee.

Policy, equity, and community

A lot of my work has happened outside a lab: in a legislator's office, in student government, in residence halls across campus, and in my community in New Jersey.

  • Pennsylvania House Health Committee: drafted legislation and recruited hearing speakers as an intern for Rep. Dan Frankel.
  • Pitt Student Government Board: chaired the Anti-Hate Ad-Hoc Committee and joined the ACC Advocacy Days delegation.
  • You & M.E.I.: co-founded a menstrual equity initiative that grew from a two-dorm pilot to every residence hall at Pitt, reaching more than 6,000 students.
  • Where it started: co-founded a student-led community health organization in high school, and used a grant to give 100 reusable period packs to local shelters.
