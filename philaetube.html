PhilaeTube

A chest tube dislodgment prevention system

Context
Bioengineering senior design, University of Pittsburgh. Fall 2025 – Spring 2026.
My role
Need-finding coordination, then design lead for the one-way breakaway valve.
With
My senior design team, including A. Kulkarni, R. Deepak, and M. Sun. The WISER simulation center hosted our ethnography.

01

The need

Chest tubes in critical care patients are often dislodged, which can lead to tension pneumothorax, hemothorax, and infection. Sutures, the current standard for securing them, don't reliably stop a tube from being pulled out by accident. We set out to secure chest tubes better, with the goals of reducing tissue trauma, avoiding emergency reinsertions, and shortening hospital stays caused by those complications.

The first phase centered on identifying and verifying a clinical need through an extensive ethnography plan. I coordinated communications with WISER and led my team through rapid brainstorming. I also designed our poster for the course's brainstorming session, where I pitched our clinical need to engineering and nursing students.

What we built

  • An adhesive anchor and retainer, modified from the StatLock style, to stabilize the thoracic tube.
  • A translucent compressive wrap to limit tube movement while keeping the site visible.
  • A breakaway tube that prevents internal dislodgment of the proximal tube.
  • A one-way pressure valve at the breakaway point. This was my part of the design.

02

Thinking like a designer

In design, my strengths are rapid, low-resolution prototyping and "killer experiments": quick tests meant to break an idea early. As design lead for the one-way valve that would sit at the breakaway joint, I started with whatever was on hand.

03

Thinking like an engineer

As the fall semester went on, I moved into medium-resolution prototyping in PLA. I also began defining the product design specifications, mapping user requirements to engineering requirements, drafting the risk analysis, and writing the verification and validation plan for the valve.

04

The key design change

In spring 2026, informal verification testing showed that our check valve didn't prevent retrograde flow.

Before we froze the design, I proposed replacing the valve mechanism with a laser-cut piece of silicone sewn in place, like a button.

05

Verification & validation

With the design frozen, we moved into formal testing against our acceptance criteria.

VER-003 · Ultimate breakaway force

Axial separation force test from ISO 80369-20:2024. I ran it with A. Kulkarni on 11 units, using a 500 N force gauge and string.

Acceptance: 15–25 N

VER-008 · Chemical resistance

Resistance to disinfectants per ASTM D543, tested by wiping the valve with alcohol.

Acceptance: withstand 10 cleaning cycles

VAL-001 · Physician validation

Tests whether physicians can set up the device correctly without critical errors.

Acceptance: 0 critical errors, ≥90% correct workflow, mean setup ≤5 min

06

What I took from it

This project ran the full arc for me: identifying a need with clinicians and nursing students, building rough prototypes fast, writing the specifications and risk analysis, and finally being willing to change the mechanism when testing showed it didn't work.
